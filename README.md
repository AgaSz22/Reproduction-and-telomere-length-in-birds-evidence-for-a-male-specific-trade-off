# Reproduction and telomere length in birds evidence for a male specific trade off
Telomeres are used as biomarkers for senescence, individual quality and somatic costs of reproduction. Here, we used them to better understand the physiological factors driving variation in reproductive output. We conducted a meta-analysis on the association between telomere dynamics and proxies of reproductive output among and within individuals in correlational studies. We found no overall association between telomere dynamics and reproductive output in birds. However, there was a signature of sex, where reproductive output negatively covaried with telomere length in males; this relationship was marginal when accounting for the age of individuals. We found no evidence of publication bias in the studies and no decline of the effect size over time. Our results suggest that any possible somatic costs of reproduction in both sexes may be obscured by differences in individual quality, which cannot be accounted for in observational studies. We recommend experimental studies combined with the manipulation of reproductive output to infer causality, to quantify the costs of reproduction and to assess individual quality.
