# Reproduction and telomere length in birds: evidence for a male specific trade off
Telomeres are increasingly used as biomarkers of senescence, individual quality and somatic costs of reproduction. Here, we used them to better understand the physiological factors driving variation in reproductive output. We conducted a meta-analysis on the association between telomere dynamics and proxies of reproductive output among and within individuals in non-manipulative correlational studies. We found no overall association between telomere dynamics and reproductive output in birds. Interestingly, there was a sex-specific pattern, where reproductive output negatively covaried with telomere length in males but not in females; however this relationship was reduced when accounting for the age of the individuals. We found no evidence of publication bias in the studies and no decline of the effect size over time. Our results suggest that any possible somatic costs of reproduction in both sexes may be obscured by differences in individual quality, which cannot be accounted for in observational studies. We recommend experimental studies combined with the manipulation of reproductive output to infer causality, to quantify the costs of reproduction, and to assess variation in individual quality.
